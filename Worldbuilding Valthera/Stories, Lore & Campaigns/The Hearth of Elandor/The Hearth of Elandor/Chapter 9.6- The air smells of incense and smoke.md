# Chapter 9.6: The air smells of incense and smoke.
Scene Setup: The Resistance Hideout
Location:
The dwarven fortress-mine deep beneath Tafalla. The party returns after Draal’s death to find the air
tense — guards whisper, and Camillia is waiting in the central chamber.
Beside her stands a towering red dragonborn in an immaculate crimson coat.
Two armed enforcers linger at his flanks, silent, armored, confident.
The air smells of incense and smoke.
The forges crackle — no one moves.
“Ah. The heroes of Tafalla. The ones who kill inquisitors and think that makes them saints.”
“How… charming.”

Yuri Volkov – The Crimson Fang
• Appearance: Impeccable, tall, scarred, his red scales dulled like bloodstained steel.
• Demeanor: Calm, cold, in full control — the predator who doesn’t need to raise his voice.
• Aura: Everyone can feel it — his presence commands the room like a general and a godfather
both.
• Pendant: A dull black gem set in gold — the Sigil of Yaga’s Oath. When he touches it, faint
script glows around his wrist — the glyph of Geas.

“You’ve made quite a mess. The Valar are furious. Draal’s body is still smoldering in the
square.
And the word ‘resistance’ is on everyone’s lips.”
“But here’s the problem: the Valar are about to discover this place… and all of you.
I can make sure that doesn’t happen.”
He flicks a gloved hand toward the table — a sealed document, bearing the crest of the Valar
Theocracy.
It’s an intelligence order naming the dwarven fortress as a “heretical stronghold” to be purged
within the week.
Then, the offer:
“You hand them over to me — my debtors, my thieves, my little troublemakers — and I make
this order disappear.
You keep your revolution. I keep my revenge.”
“Refuse… and I’ll make sure the Valar march on you tomorrow.”
He toys with the pendant in his palm.
“I am not asking. I am offering mercy.”

Act II – The Negotiation
Yuri’s ultimate goal is not the party — it’s leverage.
He wants the Lantern of Nash Retah, or failing that, to tie the party’s fate to his through Geas so he
can use them later.
He offers three possible “deals”:
1. Deliverance:
“You swear to me — one job, one retrieval. The Lantern. You bring it to me, and I protect
Tafalla.”
(If accepted, he uses Geas to enforce “Do not betray the pact.”)
2. Exile:
“Leave Tafalla now. Never return. I’ll tell the Valar the resistance died in the fire.”
(Spares Tafalla but exiles the party. Yuri gains political advantage.)
3. Refusal:
“Then the fire will come. And I’ll make sure you watch it burn.”
(Triggers the next quest — a Valar purge raid on the hideout.)

Act III – The Consequences
If the Party Accepts the Deal
• Yuri seals the pact with Geas; the glyphs burn faintly across their forearms.
• He leaves with a half-smile:
“You see? Business is easy when everyone keeps their word.”
• The resistance survives — but Yuri now knows the location of every cell.
• Future consequence: Yuri can invoke the Geas in future scenes (e.g., to compel them to kill an
ally, or deliver the Lantern).

If They Refuse
• Yuri’s eyes flicker with red light. He pockets the document.
“Then you’ve chosen faith over survival. Admirable. Stupid.”
• Within two nights, the Valar launch a coordinated purge — Vicar Lucien Vorn’s troops descend
on Tafalla.
• The next act becomes a defensive siege scenario, where the dwarven fortress must hold out.

If They Bluff or Trick Him
• Clever PCs may use magic, false deals, or counter-geas (e.g., Counterspell, Insight + Deception)
to feign agreement.
• If fooled, Yuri departs — but he’ll learn of the deception later, sending Irina Volkov or Gregor
Chainhand to hunt them down.
• Reward: They keep Tafalla safe and avoid enslavement, but gain Yuri as a long-term nemesis.

Hooks and Foreshadowing

Hooks and Foreshadowing
• The Pendant of Geas glows faintly with Null-Arcanum energy — perhaps the Valar crafted it.
• Yuri’s agents mention “a contact in Dalmerun” — tying him to the Valar nobility.
• He drops a subtle hint that the Lantern of Nash Retah is part of a “larger design”:
“Do you know why the Valar call it sacred? Because they’re too afraid to admit who built it.”
