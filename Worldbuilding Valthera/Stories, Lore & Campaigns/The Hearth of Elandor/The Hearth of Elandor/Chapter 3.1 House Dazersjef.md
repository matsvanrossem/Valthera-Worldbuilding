# Chapter 3.1 House Dazersjef
The Unsent Letter to Katarin
1. A Hidden Ledger in Her Father’s Study
Among the records of debts and false healers, Katarin finds a symbol sketched absentmindedly in the margins: a ring
of interwoven, eye-like shapes, almost resembling tentacles. It's the same shape she remembers seeing in the
Shepherds of the Meek's underground sanctum.
Next to it, her father had written a cryptic note:
"Even the dreamless ones promise salvation, but at what cost?"
Did he know? Or was it a passing warning from one of the charlatans he met?

2. Her Mother’s Dream Journal
Her mother’s entries grow increasingly disjointed as they near the end, her writing wavering, fragmented—almost
as if she had begun to dream while awake.
One passage stands out:
"He promised me a place where burdens fade, where sickness is forgotten. He stands just beyond the veil, waiting
with open arms, whispering of rest. I want to believe him. I want to sleep."
The last sentence is scratched out so violently that it tears the page, as though she changed her mind at the last
moment.

3. A Child’s Drawing, Tucked Away
An old drawing from Katarin’s childhood still lingers in the house. But something is wrong—it is not quite as she
remembers. The house, the trees, the river… they are all there. But shadows creep at the edges of the page, drawn
in a shaky, unfamiliar hand. And within the river, twisting below the surface, there is a shape —a tangled mass of
tentacles, half-erased as if someone tried to remove it but failed.
In the corner of the page, in faded ink, someone had written:
"Some dreams are not our own."

4. A Map with Faded Ink
The circled mark in the west is accompanied by faint, almost invisible scratches in the parchment. Under certain
light,
"west, where the land is warm Where the wine flows freely."
Katarin sees what appears to be writing in a language she does not know, but instinctively understands.
"South, where the veil is thin. Where he waits beyond waking."
It makes no sense—but then, neither do dreams.

5. The Locked Cabinet with an Unsent Letter
Inside the final letter meant for her, Katarin finds a smudge of ink at the bottom—at first appearing to be an
accident. But as she turns the paper, she sees that the ink has pooled into a symbol she knows too well:
A perfect black circle, empty in the center, ringed by half-formed, sleeping eyes.
And beneath it, in her mother’s delicate script, a final plea:
"Do not follow the whispers. Do not dream too deeply. Find us before they do."
These small traces suggest that her parents, in their desperation, brushed against the knowledge of Drastagha
without fully understanding it. Whether they fled because of financial ruin or because something else began
creeping into their dreams… Katarin can only wonder.

Found in a locked cabinet, its edges worn from restless handling. The ink is slightly smudged,
as if someone hesitated before sealing it away.
My Dearest Yekaterina,
I do not know if you will ever read this. I do not know if we will still be here when you return. I
pray you never need to find this letter at all.
We looked for you. Every day, every night, every prayer, every coin wasted—always hoping,
always searching, always waiting for a word, a sign, a whisper of your name. But we were too
late. You left, and in doing so, you saved us from ourselves. I see that now.
We should have let you go with love. Instead, we clung to hope until it poisoned us.
We spent all we had chasing after promises—miracles that never came, whispers from those
who only wished to take from us. But sometimes… sometimes, I wonder if something did
answer.
Do you remember when you were young, how you would wake in the night crying, claiming
someone had spoken to you in your dreams? I dismissed it as a child’s imagination. But now, I,
too, have dreamed. And the voice I hear is gentle. He does not command, nor demand. He
only waits.
Your father hears it, too. We do not speak of it aloud, but I see the exhaustion in his eyes, the
way he no longer fights sleep, only falls into it as if surrendering.
It would be so easy to follow. To lay down this burden.
But I have seen the truth in moments between waking and sleep. The empty halls. The still air.
The silence that is too deep.
This is no god of mercy.
That is why we have left, my love. There is nothing for us here anymore. We go west, where
perhaps dreams do not reach.
If you find this, do not follow.
Do not listen to the whispers. Do not seek the voice in the dark. Do not dream too deeply.
I love you, always.
—Mother
(P.S. Your father wanted to write too, but you know how he is with words. Instead, he left
something for you in the old music box. I hope it still plays.)
