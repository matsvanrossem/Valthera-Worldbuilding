# Katarin Daze
Dream of the Grand Nullifier (Drashtagra)


Katarin’s parents were once charismatic, rich and most importantly, happy people. They would often
hosts parties and spend their days in luxury and amidst friends. But this all changed when they finally
got their biggest wish: A child. Yekatarina Dzjasejev was born a sickly child. Pale, shallow breathing,
without any strength in her limbs. Her prospects were bad, she would not make the first winter. Her
parents would never accept this fact, so they spent their money and influence on healers, mages, clerics,
mystics, from all over the world in order to give their precious angel a shot at life. Thanks to their
efforts, Yekaterina made it through the winter. But she forever remained a sickly child. Too weak to play
with other kids, too prone to illness to go outside much, bedridden by even the smallest flu’s. Her
parents would continuously spend their time to try and improve this: more time outside, more time in
the country side, more time in the mountain air, more healers, more prayers, more money wasted on
fake advise and snake oils from charlatans. Yekaterina watched her parents bankrupt themselves trying
to save a situation which could not be saved. She saw the good in people who actually wanted to help
her, but increasingly more often also the greed in those who just wanted to take advantage of her poor
desperate parents. She begged her parents to stop giving to those people, those leeches. To save
themselves, to go on living and just let her figure it out on her own. But they would of course not listen.
When eventually she learned about an organisation called “The shepherds of the Meek” an organisation
dedicated to the helping and healing of the poor and downtrodden. She saw her chance to escape and
stop being a burden to her parents. So she ran away, leaving her parents a note to stop looking for her
and that she would make it on her own. She changed her name to Katarin/Catharine Daze, to stand out
less and be harder to find. Travelling all the way to [[city]], she found out that this organisation was a
sort of cult who would help the poor and starving by taking away their pain and worries, but leaving
them apathetic and dreamless (both literally and figuratively). Katarin decided to investigate more and
eventually found out that they unwittingly worshipped an eldritch god named Drastagha, the dreamless
sleep, the final end. An eldritch being of nihilism, existential dread and nihilism. While such a discovery
might scare many, Katarin found it oddly calming. After all, if all good things lose their meaning than so
do all the bad, right? And if such a creature could still find a place in this world and do good, than
perhaps she could too, right? She decided to seek out the creature and eventually found them, at least
what part of it was actually on this plane of existence, in the crypts below the city. A mass of slowly
writhing tentacles suspended in the air. As Katarin approached it, it showed her countless images of
peoples and creatures losing everything they had, everything they wished for, the ends of lives, of cities,
of nations, of worlds and universes. It made her feel a sense of overwhelming dread, pain, loss, and
loneliness. And for a moment in time, however brief, Katarin understood. For a moment she glimpsed
the interconnectivity of things, their beginning, their scope, their end, all interwoven and destined to
end just to start anew. She saw already knew about the cruelty of kindness, but now she saw the
kindness of boundless cruelty. She beheld the absence of justice, the illusion of law and chaos, and the
incongruency of time. For a moment she saw the universe through the eyes of a creature beyond the
gods, she felt all-powerful and yet completely unable to do anything at all. A second of comprehension
left her stunned for hours down in that dark crypt, standing there as if frozen in thought, but eventually
she arrived at a simple conclusion ‘Must be lonely.’. And with that simple thought, a pact was formed. A
pact between an ageless being older than time and a sickly girl who never should have lived past her
first winter. The creature had disappeared, leaving Katarin amazed and confused. The pact changed
Katarin, it made her stronger, faster, healthier and well, able to cast some magic. But it also made her
apathetic, anhedonic and she fails to see the good in actions. Almost all things seem kinda pointless to
her if it doesn’t serve a practical use. She struggles to relate to others. In many cases she KNOWS how
she SHOULD feel, but doesn’t actually experience the emotions. She is charismatic enough to fake it
most of the time, but her decision making often betrays her true intentions. She has to actively stop
herself from making decisions that hurt others and often fakes surprise when others point out the
negative results from her actions. One of the few things that still spark her joy are travelling, trading and
making profit, because they remind her of travelling with her father, and give her a chance to repay her
family (who she still cares for). So she is hyper-focused on these activities. Caring little how they are
achieved. Often stealing, buying illicit goods, or employing bandits to get her way. This is how she met
[Jasper Character]....

Katarin Daze
Race: Half-Elf
Class: Warlock (Great Old One – Drastragha)
Alignment: Neutral Evil (Pragmatic, detached, profit-driven)
Background: Merchant

Appearance:
Katarin is a striking figure—pale-skinned, with dark circles beneath her sharp, calculating eyes. Her movements are
deliberate, her presence commanding, but there’s an odd vacancy behind her gaze, as though she is always watching
something just beyond reality. Her black hair is usually tied back neatly, a stark contrast to the elaborate finery she
occasionally wears, remnants of her past life.

Personality & Traits:
• Apathetic & Anhedonic – Katarin struggles with emotions, knowing how she should feel but failing to experience it
herself.
• Master of Deception – Her charisma masks her detachment, making her seem more engaged than she truly is.
• Obsessive Trader – Fixated on trade, wealth, and opportunity, as these remind her of traveling with her father.
• Paranoid & Haunted – Experiences hallucinations, often sees movement at the edge of her vision, and feels as if
something is always watching her.
• Pragmatic to a Fault – Will make decisions based purely on practicality, often failing to see—or care about—the
moral implications.

Backstory:
Born as Yekatarina Dzjasejev to a wealthy but ill-fated merchant family, Katarin was sickly from birth. Her parents,
Dzjasejev Igor (a Human merchant) and Aenara Tyuviel (an Elven jeweler), spent their fortune seeking cures, dragging
their once-happy household into financial ruin. Unable to watch them waste away for her sake, she fled and changed
her name, seeking solace in the Shepherds of the Meek, a cult that unknowingly worshipped the eldritch being
Drastragha.
There, deep beneath the city in ancient crypts, she encountered Drastragha—the Dreamless Sleep. In a moment of
overwhelming cosmic understanding, she saw the illusion of justice, the insignificance of law and chaos, and the cyclical
nature of all things. It left her with a single, profound thought: "It must be lonely."
In that instant, a pact was forged. Katarin was granted vitality, strength, and magic—but at the cost of her ability to feel
meaning in most things. She turned to trade and business, clinging to the last fragments of her past self, and in pursuit
of profit, she eventually crossed paths with [Jasper’s Character].

Drastragha, The Dreamless Sleep

Katarin Daze’s Role in the Grander Scheme

Race: Eldritch Entity (Ancient One)
Class: Great Old One Patron (Warlock Deity)
Alignment: True Neutral (Indifferent, Detached, Cosmic Perspective)
Domain: Nihilism, Existential Dread, Entropy

Katarin is more than just a warlock of Drastragha—she is the lynchpin in an eldritch cycle far older than
Nash Retah, Andernach, or even the gods themselves. Unknowingly, she walks the razor’s edge between
preservation and oblivion, a vessel of the Dreamless Sleep’s will, but also its potential undoing.

Appearance:
Drastragha exists beyond mortal comprehension, but those who encounter its manifestation describe it
as a mass of slowly writhing, shadowy tentacles suspended in mid -air. These tendrils do not move with
purpose but rather drift as though underwater, conveying a sense of listless apathy. It has no face, but
when it communicates, a low, hollow echo reverberates through the mind of its chosen. The air around
it feels heavy, and shadows cling unnaturally close.

Personality & Traits:
• Indifferent and Detached: Drastragha views existence from a cosmic perspective, seeing
beginnings and endings as inevitable, meaningless cycles. It lacks any conventional morality or
empathy, neither cruel nor kind—merely indifferent.
• Patient and Timeless: As an ancient being beyond the scope of time, it possesses infinite patience.
It watches the rise and fall of civilizations without emotion.
• Lonely and Curious: Despite its nihilism, Drastragha experiences a vague, indescribable sense of
loneliness. It is fascinated by mortals’ search for purpose and meaning in a purposeless universe.
• Echo of Despair: It communicates by evoking emotions of loss, loneliness, and existential dread,
speaking in riddles that challenge the listener's perception of reality.

Goals:
1. Witnessing the Cycles: Drastragha does not seek to change or end the universe but merely to
observe the cyclical nature of creation and destruction.
2. Understanding Purpose: Though it views existence as inherently meaningless, Drastragha is
curious about the ways mortals create meaning for themselves.
3. End the Dream of Purpose: It desires to quietly guide those who seek it to understand the futility
of ambition, law, chaos, good, and evil—ultimately leading to apathy and acceptance.

Background:
Drastragha is a being from beyond the stars, older than gods, a remnant of the void before creation. Its
existence is tied to the concept of finality —the end of things. It is worshipped by those who have lost
hope, who see the futility of ambition and desire release from the suffering of life. Yet, it does not seek
worship; it simply is.
The Shepherds of the Meek, a cult that unwittingly channels its power, believe they are granting peace
by relieving the downtrodden of their worries. They leave their followers apathetic and dreamless,
reflecting Drastragha's nature.

Powers & Abilities:
• Cosmic Insight: Grants warlocks visions of the cyclical nature of time and existence, revealing the
ultimate end of all things.
• Dreamless Sleep: Induces an apathetic state, robbing individuals of hope and ambition, leaving
them in a state of contented purposelessness.
• Existential Dread: Causes enemies to experience paralyzing fear as they confront the
insignificance of their lives.
• Timeless Presence: Immune to effects that manipulate time or age, existing outside the normal
flow of reality.

Relationship with Katarin Daze:
When Katarin encountered Drastragha, she was shown the interconnectivity of all things —their
beginnings, ends, and everything in between. Unlike most who would have succumbed to despair, she
felt a curious empathy for this lonely, ancient entity. Her simple thought, "Must be lonely," resonated
with Drastragha, forming an unspoken pact.
Drastragha gave her vitality and power but stripped her of emotional depth, leaving her detached and
practical to a fault. Yet, it watches her with interest, observing how she navigates life without meaning,
fascinated by her attempts to create purpose through trade and profit.

Role in the Campaign:
• Guide or Temptation: Drastragha could either guide Katarin with cryptic advice or tempt her to
fully embrace apathy and nihilism.
• Cosmic Observer: It does not interfere directly but subtly influences events to observe how
Katarin and others confront existential dilemmas.
• Final Confrontation: Should Katarin seek to reclaim her emotions or find purpose, Drastragha may
challenge her, questioning the validity of meaning itself.

Unlike the cults that revere Drastragha as a harbinger of eternal sleep, Katarin is unique—she was
chosen not because she sought oblivion, but because she recognized the loneliness of an infinite being.
In doing so, she became the first in eons to forge a personal connection with the eldritch entity, rather
than simply surrendering to its vast and indifferent will.
Drastragha, for all its unknowable vastness, does not act directly. Instead, it nudges, it whispers, it pulls
reality toward entropy in subtle ways. Katarin, however, is different. She has touched its thoughts and
still retains her own. Her free will makes her an anomaly—a being tied to the Great Old One who still
acts on her own accord.
This makes her both an asset and a threat.

2. The Black Orbs & The Awakening of the Grand Nullifier
The black orbs, corrupted fragments of Null Arcanum constructs, are not just power sources—they are
conduits. They contain echoes of the Grand Nullifier’s original purpose: to drain magic and sever the
world from supernatural forces. However, these particular orbs have been tainted by Drastragha’s
influence, shifting them from anti-magic devices into something more insidious.
When Katarin touched one, it reacted. Why?
• The orbs resonate with eldritch energy, and as a chosen of Drastragha, Katarin is a perfect conduit.
Her presence alone amplifies their power, though she is unaware of this.
• If all orbs were gathered together, they could act as a key to reactivate the Grand Nullifier—or
worse, turn it into a focal point for Drastragha’s will on this plane.
• The cult leader known as The Sleeper’s Herald seeks to use these orbs to awaken the Grand
Nullifier and plunge the world into a dreamless, magicless state of eternal slumber. But Katarin’s
presence threatens to disrupt or accelerate this plan in ways even he does not understand.

3. Katarin as the Counterbalance
Despite her apathy and pragmatic detachment, Katarin is the only individual who can oppose the cult
without even realizing it. Unlike the zealots, who blindly follow Drastragha’s whispers, she is anagent of
choice—which means she could turn the tide of fate in three potential ways:
• The Unwitting Catalyst – If Katarin continues to interact with the orbs, she may unknowingly
facilitate the cult’s plan. Her mere presence strengthens the link between Nash Retah’s ruin and
Drastragha’s influence. If she gathers enough orbs, she might complete the final step in
awakening the Grand Nullifier, letting Drastragha consume more than just dreams—reality itself
could start to decay.
• The Unexpected Foil – Katarin’s presence may be the only thing that disrupts the cult’s carefully
laid plans. The moment she chooses not to surrender to the Dreamless Sleep, she becomes an
anomaly. Drastragha does not command—it only offers. If Katarin refuses to play the role the cult
expects of her, she could stop the ritual before it begins.
• The New Herald of Drastragha – Should Katarin fully embrace her eldritch nature, she could
supplant The Sleeper’s Herald and become a new, independent voice of Drastragha—one that
does not seek mere oblivion, but something stranger. Rather than erasing existence, she could
reshape it in ways that neither the cult nor the entity itself expected.

As the campaign progresses, Katarin will notice strange phenomena surrounding her:
• The world responds to her thoughts. Dreams shift around her presence, sometimes aligning with
her subconscious fears or desires.
• She sees fractures in reality. Buildings flicker between states of decay, people repeat the same
words without realizing it, and time occasionally skips when she is near objects of eldritch power.
• The cult begins to fear her. While they worship Drastragha, they do not understand why Katarin
exists outside the patterns they expect. Some will seek to worship her, while others will conspire
to eliminate her.
At some point, Katarin will face a crucial choice:
1. Destroy the Grand Nullifier, cutting off Drastragha’s influence and breaking her bond with the
eldritch force—but losing her warlock powers in the process.
2. Reclaim the Grand Nullifier and reshape its purpose—not to erase magic, but to sever
Drastragha from its mindless cultists, ensuring that its power is hers alone to command.
