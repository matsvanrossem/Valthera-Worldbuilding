# Main Campaign Log

## Episode 1: Introduction

### Act 1: Abandon Ship!! Finding Life in Elandor

The party begins aboard the **Unyielding Justice**, a Valar prison vessel carrying prisoners, dissidents, and secret cargo. Captain Ashka “Ironclaw” and the Crimson Tide attack during a violent storm, turning the voyage into a three-way disaster between prisoners, Valar soldiers, and raiders.

The ship breaks apart in the storm. The party escapes into a lifeboat or wreckage and eventually reaches the coast near **Andernach**, entering Elandor with little gear, money, or security. The opening establishes the Valar Theocracy as a major threat and hints that Null-Arcanum cargo aboard the ship was more important than the prisoners were told.

### Act 2: First Steps in Andernach

The party struggles to survive in Andernach’s Lower Wards and begins building contacts. Early opportunities include work through **Garrik “Ironbrand” Volstag**, beast-hunting jobs from **Tobin “The Tender” Halloway**, and increasingly political work connected to **Lady Seraphine D’Aleron**.

The early Andernach arc introduces street crime, smugglers, missing couriers, bounty work, and the first signs that unusual magical activity is spreading around the city.

### Chapter 2.1: The Brackwater Fenn

Tobin directs the party toward threats in the Brackwater Fenn. At **Saltwitch’s Hollow**, locals report missing livestock, enormous tracks, warped wildlife, and a mutated dire toad or swamp predator.

After the creature is dealt with, signs point to a larger problem. Arcane sigils, unnatural mutations, and old wards suggest that the swamp creatures are symptoms of something deeper rather than isolated monsters.

### Act 3: That What Stirs in Andernach

The party discovers ritual circles, Null-Arcanum residue, mutilated wildlife, and evidence that someone is deliberately experimenting on local creatures. Tobin believes Andernach itself will eventually be threatened if the source is not stopped.

The investigation begins connecting the Brackwater mutations to smuggling routes, lost relics from **Nash Retah**, and a figure or cult associated with **the Sleeper’s Herald**.

### Chapter 3.1: House Dazersjef

Katarin’s family history becomes entangled with the larger mystery. Letters, dream journals, drawings, and old family records imply that her parents encountered the influence of **Drastagha** long before the current crisis.

The material suggests that Katarin’s childhood dreams and the family’s later desperation may have been connected to the same force now surfacing through the orbs and the Sleeper’s cult.

### Chapter 3.2: The Ebon Tide

The party investigates the ship **Ebon Tide** and corrupted black orbs being moved through Andernach. The ship’s captain shows signs of mental corruption, and cargo records indicate that the orbs have been distributed through criminal and noble channels.

The party learns that the orbs are not merely valuable relics. They are part of a much larger mechanism or ritual, and multiple factions are searching for them.

### Act 4: A Noble’s Secret

Lady Seraphine reveals that someone among Andernach’s nobility has been purchasing relics from Nash Retah. An assassination attempt during a discreet meeting confirms that the conspiracy reaches into the city’s upper classes.

The party is pushed toward the Valar religious hierarchy, hidden buyers, the Sleeper’s cult, and the secret history of the orbs.

### Chapter 4.1: The Cathedral of Saint Avarr, the Valar Sanctum

The party targets the fortified Valar Sanctum beneath or within Andernach’s cathedral. The complex contains inquisitorial records, prisoners, Null-Arcanum weapons, and a hidden vault containing the orbs.

At the deepest level, the party can confront **Archbishop Kessara Valore** and discover that the orbs were components of an ancient celestial or arcane machine associated with Nash Retah. Valore warns that the Sleeper’s Herald and an abandoned temple outside the city are part of a much greater threat.

### Chapter 4.2: Subplot, Blood and Gold

The campaign expands into the **Volkov Syndicate** and its hidden records. The **Blood Ledger** becomes a major object of leverage, containing debts, killings, blackmail, and other secrets tied to Yuri Volkov and his network.

This subplot links criminal power, noble politics, and later choices involving Yuri.

### Chapter 4.3: Finding Out Who Baba Yaga Is

The investigation broadens into older supernatural powers and hidden identities. This material serves as a bridge between the Andernach conspiracy, the dream-related threat, and the more mythic forces that begin to dominate later episodes.

### Act 5: The Ball at House Thornvale

A major masked ball at **House Thornvale** brings the campaign’s political and supernatural threads together. Nobles, spies, criminal agents, and cult-linked figures mingle while the party pursues multiple personal and factional objectives.

**Zack** encounters **Veridian Thornvale**, who is linked to the demon **Ravorex**, an ancient rival of Bel. The ball also exposes Volkov intrigue, further clues about the orbs and the Sleeper, and possible routes toward the arcane prison beneath Andernach.

The act closes with the sense that several separate conflicts are actually converging: the Valar, the Volkov Syndicate, the orbs, the Sleeper’s cult, Ravorex, and Nash Retah.

## Episode 2: Resistance

### Act 6: The Dreamweft Hollow

The party enters a dream-corrupted cult complex in the Brackwater Fenns. Victims are trapped in half-sleep, dreams are harvested as fuel, memories become unreliable, and the cult’s chambers increasingly distort perception.

At the center are dream conduits, an unstable orb, and cultists transformed by Drastagha’s influence. The dungeon reveals direct visions of **Nash Retah** and suggests that the orbs are keys to something buried there.

### Chapter 6.2: Omens of Nash Retah

Cult leaders recognize Katarin as a significant figure in Drastagha’s design, calling her a lost herald or “Mind-Walker.” The party can fight, bargain with, deceive, or manipulate the cult.

The **Dreamer’s Well** offers dangerous knowledge about the cult’s goals, the orbs, and Nash Retah. The chapter strengthens Katarin’s personal connection to Drastagha and the approaching crisis.

### Act 7: Taint on the Road

The Valar begin moving the orbs under heavy divine escort. Their containment is failing, and even the guards experience dream symptoms and mental instability.

The party may intercept the convoy, steal one or more orbs, let the cult strike first, or become entangled with the Valar escort. A containment failure causes an orb to react violently and project messages or memories linked to Nash Retah.

### Chapter 7.1: Escaping the Valar Once Again

If the party takes the orbs, they become hunted fugitives. The relics themselves act like beacons and begin corrupting dreams, revealing fragments of Nash Retah’s fall and the activation of the **Grand Nullifier**.

The party is forced to choose routes, allies, and hiding places while deciding whether to expose the Valar, seek help in Dalmerun, bargain with cultists, or push directly toward Nash Retah.

### Act 8: Arrival in Dalmerun

The party reaches **Dalmerun, the River Crown**, where the campaign shifts into a larger political arena. Possible approaches include seeking legal protection from the Ledgersmen, spying on the Inquisition, investigating the Riverbone Ward, appearing before the Guild Council, or following personal leads.

Rumors of dreamless sleepers, reactivated chapels, hidden Null-Arcanum records, and growing Valar influence show that the capital is already being affected by the same crisis.

### Chapter 8.1: A Lich’s Bargain

Beneath Dalmerun lies the buried city of **Rakash** and the lich **Maltherion Veythar**, an ancient necromancer who hates the Valar. He is presented less as a simple monster and more as a powerful political wildcard.

At the same time, evidence from Tafalla reaches Alejandro. Tafalla has been occupied and converted into a Valar Null-Arcanum refinery. The resistance is weakening, Brother Draal and the Burning Choir are active, and the party is drawn west toward Alejandro’s former home.

### Act 9: The Journey to Tafalla

The party travels from Dalmerun through the heartlands, Silverwyre territory, Heatror Pass, and the western borderlands toward occupied Tafalla. The trip is designed as a long campaign journey with political encounters, exploration, Valar patrols, refugees, dream phenomena, and warnings about the Burning Choir.

They learn that a mystical **Lantern** hangs in Tafalla’s square as a symbol of Valar victory. In truth, it is tied to Nash Retah’s sealing rituals, Maltherion, and the Grand Nullifier.

### Chapter 9.1: The Khazruth Deep

Marked in the notes as **skipped**. The planned material concerns the Restless Kings, an ancient dwarven hold, and a river of fire beneath the mountains.

### Chapter 9.2: The Resistance

The party makes contact with the resistance inside Tafalla and begins operating against the occupation. The campaign introduces infiltration routes, prisoners, Null-Arcanum restraints, Valar records, and resistance safehouses.

The resistance needs practical victories before it can challenge the Valar openly.

### Chapter 9.3: Quest 1, Sister Avelina’s Experiments

The party raids a chapel disguised as a hospital but actually used as a torture and experimentation site. Their objective is to break the Null-Chains, free prisoners, and rescue **Kesta Varrin**.

Success undermines Sister Avelina and provides a lead to artificer **Soriya Varrin**, whose work can counter Valar anti-magic technology.

### Chapter 9.4: Quest 2, Fire Sleeps Beneath Tafalla

Kesta directs the party to Soriya, who is hiding in the **Old Silverworks** beneath Tafalla. Soriya was once an artificer of Elandor’s royal forge guild and can create weapons and equipment that still function inside anti-magic zones.

The party must reach her before Vicar Lucien Vorn’s agents do. Her work could turn the resistance from a scattered rebellion into a force capable of fighting the Valar directly.

### Chapter 9.5: Quest 3, When the Bell Tolls, the Fire Answers

Brother **Draal** stages public executions in Tafalla’s square to crush resistance morale. The party can turn the execution into an uprising and confront Draal in front of the population.

If Draal falls, the victory becomes a symbol that the Valar can be resisted. The occupation responds by escalating, with Vicar Lucien Vorn and other higher-ranking enemies becoming more directly involved.

### Chapter 9.6: The Air Smells of Incense and Smoke

The resistance begins dismantling Vorn’s illusion and informant network. False appearances, manipulated civilians, and hidden agents make it difficult to know who is actually betraying Tafalla.

Breaking the network gives the resistance breathing room but also makes the conflict more personal, because Vorn now knows who is responsible.

### Chapter 9.7: Quest 4, Every Voice in Tafalla Speaks in Two Tongues

Paranoia spreads through the city. The resistance knows that spies are feeding information to the Valar, but some suspected collaborators are innocent people altered by illusion or manipulation.

The party must identify the real informants without destroying the resistance from within.

### Chapter 9.8: Lantern’s Shadow

This is the mainline climax of the Tafalla arc. Draal is dead, Avelina is gone or defeated, Vorn’s network has been damaged, and the Valar’s control is weakening.

The Lantern awakens like a second sun above Tafalla. The resistance believes that if the associated ritual succeeds, the city will be consumed by holy fire. The party must seize, stop, redirect, or otherwise confront the Lantern before the Burning Choir can complete its purpose.

## Episode 3: Holy Crusade of Elandor / Yuri’s Offer

### Act 10: Prelude to War / Hunting a Dragon

Elandor is now preparing for open war. With the army stretched thin, the party is sent to protect a critical military supply convoy moving along the River Dalme.

The threat proves to be more than a single dragon. The encounter becomes a test of Elandor’s remaining military strength and establishes that the kingdom is cornered but still capable of organized resistance.

### Chapter 10.1: Triumph and Return

After the dragon conflict, the party returns to a kingdom moving toward full-scale war. **Yuri Volkov** offers an alternative front: hunt **Veridian Thornvale**, who is no longer merely possessed by Ravorex but increasingly aligned with him.

Yuri frames the choice as one between becoming generals and symbols in Elandor’s war or entering Nash Retah to confront a threat older than the Valar crusade. His proposed route into Nash Retah is effectively one-way.

### Chapter 10.2: The Trifold Path

The campaign branches around three connected existential threats: the Valar crusade, Ravorex and the infernal war, and the unresolved power beneath Nash Retah.

The party’s earlier alliances, bargains, and personal obligations determine which path becomes dominant and what resources they have when the conflicts converge.

### Chapter 10.3: The Dream That Refuses to End

Inside Nash Retah, the party meets **Sothair**, an ancient figure bound to the ruined land. He explains that the **Grand Nullifier** was an attempt to “unmake unmaking,” powered by a force that its creators never truly controlled.

The party reaches the remains of the Nullifier at **Aldar Ihram**, where reality, memory, and Drastagha’s influence are unstable. A shadow-version of Sothair appears, Veridian’s trail becomes clear, and Katarin’s connection to Drastagha grows stronger.

### Chapter 10.4: The Road to Aldar Irham

The party closes on the Nullifier’s core while Ravorex’s forces approach. The orbs resonate strongly with Katarin, and the campaign reveals that Ravorex’s goal is not simple destruction but the weaponization of entropy.

The party faces several strategic possibilities: complete the machine, let Ravorex secure more of its power, destroy the orbs, or redirect the device. Every route risks effects ranging from localized destruction to reality-scale catastrophe.

### Chapter 10.5: Ravorex’s Reasoning

The confrontation becomes ideological as well as physical. Ravorex’s forces begin to fracture, ancient technology and infernal power converge, and the party must decide what to do with the surviving machinery, relics, and knowledge of Aldar Ihram.

Possible consequences include returning to the war with new weapons, exposing the truth behind Valar power, changing the future of Nash Retah, reshaping personal bargains, and triggering planar fallout.

### Act 11: Aftermath of Cataclysm

The campaign returns to a devastated Elandor. The war is now being fought through collapsing cities, siege lines, revolutionary fighters, Valar shock troops, inquisitors, and dwarven allies.

The party’s individual identities are framed in mythic terms: Katarin as Finality, Nyx as Grief, Zack as Severance, Snowball/Haigha as Identity, and Alejandro as Defiance.

### Chapter 11.1: Battle of Heatror Fields

The party moves toward the Heatror front while Valar forces press the region. The chapter combines mountain travel, battlefield decisions, defense of Silverwyre positions, and threats against Nyx’s family.

The fighting escalates into a struggle over fortified positions and the survival of the remaining defenders.

### Chapter 11.2: The Avalanche Gambit

The party exploits the mountain terrain to break the Valar advance. The planned gambit uses the Heatror landscape itself as a weapon, with the risk of catastrophic collateral damage if the plan fails.

### Chapter 11.3: The Broken Siege

The avalanche destroys or scatters large portions of the Valar siege force. The Valar do not simply flee, but their organization breaks long enough to create a short window for counterattacks and strike missions.

The resistance and Silverwyre forces regroup while rumors spread that the mountain itself answered the party.

### Act 12: The Dwendalin Deep

The party found their way into the **Dwendalin Deep**, entering the immense abandoned dwarven realm beneath the mountains.

Their path carried them through **Haldrcragg**, following the old dwarven passages deeper into the mountain until they reached **the Warrens**. The Warrens formed an enormous subterranean complex of ruined infrastructure, ancient machinery, forgotten roads, and hostile depths.

Rather than attempting to clear the sprawling mega-dungeon, the party found a route that allowed them to **bypass a large portion of the Warrens**. They pressed deeper toward **Valdur Altis**, the ancient route known as **the King’s Ascent**.

Their shortcut eventually brought them to the **Altis Citadel**, where they discovered one of the great treasures of the old dwarven kingdom: **the Crown**.

The party stole the Crown from the Citadel, but taking it awakened a far greater danger. **Two ancient white dragons** confronted them within the depths.

Rather than remain and fight the dragons, the party fled.

With the Crown in their possession, they escaped the Altis Citadel and made their way out of the Dwendalin Deep, leaving most of the vast Warrens unexplored behind them.
