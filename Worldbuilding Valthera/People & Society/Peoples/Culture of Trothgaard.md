# Culture of Trothgaard

Trothgaard culture is deeply rooted in tradition and hierarchy,
with each hold having its own distinct customs and practices.
Despite their differences, the dwarves of Trothgaard all share
a love of music, festivals, and art.
#### Music

In the Hold of Mror, music is a way of life. The dwarves here
are known for their mastery of the bagpipes and drums, and
their music can be heard echoing throughout the mountains.
Mror dwarves are known for their percussive and rhythmic
music, often played on drums and anvils.They also celebrate
the annual Forgefest, a festival dedicated to engineering and
metalworking.
#### Festivals and holidays

The Erber Dwarves take great pride in their agricultural
skills and brewing. They use the unique flora and fauna of the
cave systems to create a variety of potent and flavorful
beverages. They celebrate their harvest with a festival that
includes contests for the largest and most unusual crops. In
Targen, artistry is highly valued. The dwarves here are master
stone carvers and masons, and they use their skills to create
intricate sculptures and stunning architecture. They hold an
annual art festival that showcases the best of their work and
attracts artists from all over Trothgaard. The Cairnheim
Duergar are known for their skill in jewelry-making and
metalworking. Their festivals are marked by colorful displays
of intricate, gem-studded pieces of jewelry and ornate
weapons. In the Hold of Trothgaard, where the rulers reside,
bureaucracy is highly regarded. The dwarves here are
meticulous record-keepers and writers, and they celebrate
their love of organization with a festival that includes
competitions in penmanship and accounting.
#### Education

Mror, being known for their skilled weaponsmiths and
inventors, place a heavy emphasis on engineering and
crafting skills. Children are taught basic mathematics,
geometry, and metallurgy at a young age.As they grow older,
they are trained in the use of different tools and machinery,
and encouraged to experiment with different materials and
techniques.
Erber, being the agricultural geniuses of Trothgaard, focus
on teaching their children about botany, mycology, and
horticulture. They are taught how to cultivate and care for
various plants and fungi, and how to use them in cooking,
medicine, and other applications. They also learn about
animal husbandry and breeding, and the various tools and
techniques used in farming.
Targen, being the master carvers and stone masons, teach
their children the art of stonework and sculpture. They are
taught how to mine and quarry stone, and how to shape it
into various forms using chisels, hammers, and other tools.
They also learn about architecture and design, and how to
create functional and aesthetically pleasing structures.
Cairnheim, being the master jewelers and deep underdark
dwellers, teach their children the art of gemcutting and
metallurgy. They are taught how to identify and extract
precious metals and gemstones from the earth, and how to
shape and polish them into beautiful works of art. They also
learn about metallurgy, and how to create different alloys and
metals with different properties.
Trothgaard, being the traders, lawmakers, and
bureaucrats of the nation, focus on teaching their children
about commerce, diplomacy, and governance. They learn
about economics, accounting, and trade, as well as the laws
and regulations of Trothgaard. They also learn about history
and politics, and how to negotiate and mediate between
different factions and groups.
