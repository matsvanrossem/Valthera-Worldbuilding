# Culture of Osteria

The culture of Osteria is shaped by its history and the
constant threat of the undead and necromancers that roam
the land.As a result, Osterians tend to be wary and guarded
around strangers, with a strong sense of loyalty and
community within their own cities and towns.
#### Music

One of the most notable forms of Osterian music is the
lament, a mournful song that is often used in funerals or to
express grief and sadness. The lament is typically sung in a
slow, mournful manner and is accompanied by a single
instrument or a small ensemble.Another important form of
Osterian music is the ballad, a narrative song that tells a
story. These ballads often feature themes of love, loss, and
tragedy and are usually accompanied by a lute or other
stringed instrument. One of the mayor ballads is centered
arround the great plague and the loss of life, it is called
"Echoes of the night: A ballad of Osteria"
#### Festivals and holidays

Osteria is not known for its many festivals and ceremonies
but one mayor ceremony; the Ceremony of the White Lady -
This ceremony is held in honor ofAylisha, also known as the
White Lady, who is revered as a hero for her role in the
rebellion against Ferdinand Vildamir. The ceremony includes
the lighting of candles in her honor all across Osteria.
#### Education

In Osteria, education is primarily centered around the
worship of the god Nichtengall, with his temples serving as
the main centers of learning. There, people are trained in the
arts of song and writing, as well as other skills related to
religious rituals and practices. Outside of the temples,
education is mainly in the form of apprenticeships. Young
people are often apprenticed to skilled workers in various
trades, such as blacksmithing, carpentry, or farming. These
apprenticeships can last for several years and provide
practical, hands-on experience in a particular trade.
