# The Wastes of Shorath
The Wastes of Shorath: Overview
The island spans roughly 200,000 square kilometers, making it a vast and formidable landscape. The
geography of Shorath is diverse, ranging from barren deserts and jagged mountains to dense, swampy
forests and treacherous coastal cliffs. Its strategic location—distant from the mainland and surrounded
by turbulent seas—ensures that escape is nearly impossible.

Key Regions of The Wastes of Shorath:
1. The Hollow Citadel (Capital of the Wastes):
At the heart of Shorath sits the Hollow Citadel, a massive fortress built into the side of a dormant
volcanic mountain. The Citadel functions as the central hub for the entire island’s administration,
housing the prison’s overseers, Theocratic guards, and military officials. It is heavily fortified with
Null-Arcanum to suppress any magical influence. It is here that political prisoners, former leaders,
or powerful figures are kept in solitary confinement or labor camps under constant surveillance.
The Citadel also serves as the main base for conducting patrols and suppressing any uprisings. The
harsh winds and constant ashfalls make it a nearly unbearable place, symbolizing the might of the
Theocracy.
2. The Ashen Barrens:
To the north of the Citadel, the Ashen Barrens is a vast, sun-scorched desert where little
vegetation survives. This barren wasteland is where most of the prisoners sent to Shorath are
deposited. They must either adapt to the harsh conditions or perish. Dust storms and scorching
heat make survival difficult, and the exiles are often forced to work mining the land for minerals
and digging through ancient, cratered remains that hide remnants of the Ether War. The land is
pocked with craters and jagged ridges, some remnants from the fall of the star that brought NullArcanum to the world.
3. The Gloomswamps:
In the southern part of Shorath lies The Gloomswamps, a region where the air is thick with mist
and the ground is boggy and unstable. The swamps are home to dangerous fauna, including
serpentine creatures and venomous insects. It is here that rebellious prisoners are sent to endure
the slow decay of their spirit. The swamps are often used for forced labor, where prisoners are
tasked with draining the marshes or gathering rare herbs and materials. The oppressive humidity
and stifling heat cause most prisoners to succumb to disease, exhaustion, or madness. Some
reports claim that the Gloomswamps harbor pockets of long-forgotten magical sites, though this is
denied by the Theocracy, who control access to the area with iron-fisted rule.
4. The Blackfang Mountains:
Rising in the center of the island, the Blackfang Mountains are jagged peaks that slice through the
landscape like teeth. These mountains are treacherous, with freezing winds, dangerous wildlife,
and unstable rock faces. This region is where the most dangerous criminals, heretics, and violent
rebels are sent to mine for Null-Arcanum, using primitive tools under the threat of violence. The
harsh conditions, lack of food, and constant work in the mines are designed to break the will of
the prisoners. Only the strongest survive here, though they are often left weakened, crippled, or
mentally broken. A few secretive Theocratic patrols move through the mountains, ensuring that
no one escapes or gathers information that could challenge the Theocracy's dominance.
5. The Bleak Coast:
The western edge of Shorath is home to The Bleak Coast, a series of craggy cliffs and jagged
peninsulas that extend out into the turbulent sea. This area is used for "reeducation" camps,
where prisoners are indoctrinated with the doctrines of the Valar Theocracy. It is a place for
prisoners who are thought to be useful to the Theocracy but are in need of breaking their former
loyalties or magical inclinations. Many of these camps are built on platforms that extend over the
sea, where prisoners are put to work building fortifications, bridges, or attempting dangerous
naval tasks. Those who fail or defy the Theocracy are cast into the sea, where the currents ensure

naval tasks. Those who fail or defy the Theocracy are cast into the sea, where the currents ensure
they never return.
6. The Eternal Nightlands:
In the southernmost region of Shorath, near the equator, lies The Eternal Nightlands, a place
shrouded in eternal twilight. The island is perpetually shadowed by the presence of a vast, cursed
forest of gnarled trees that block out the sun, and the region is a chilling, surreal environment. It is
here that magical exiles are sent—their power suppressed by the residual Null-Arcanum in the soil
and air. The lands are said to be haunted by twisted creatures and spectral figures, remnants of
the ancient conflicts of the Ether War. It’s the one place where sorcerers and wizards are left to
die slowly, their magical energies drained by the very land itself.
7. The Cursed Reef:
The Cursed Reef is a notorious area that encircles the eastern shores of Shorath. Covered in sharp
rocks and reef beds, the waters here are treacherous and are the site of numerous shipwrecks
from past exile ships. The Theocracy employs an advanced naval force to patrol these waters,
ensuring that any would-be escapees are caught or killed by harsh weather conditions, wild sea
creatures, or Theocratic patrols. Those who attempt to flee by sea are often met with death, and
the reef has become a symbol of ultimate despair for the exiled.

The Society of Exiles:
Life on Shorath is brutal. Most prisoners are forced to subsist off meager rations and are constantly
subject to physical labor. While a few manage to form alliances and small communities, the brutality of
the island’s layout and the ever-present threat of violence from Theocratic enforcers keeps most groups
fractured and distrustful. There are whispers that hidden communities of defiant prisoners still operate
in secret, but any who attempt rebellion are swiftly dealt with.
In this harsh world, survival depends on strength, cunning, and sometimes sheer luck. The Wastes are
not only a prison but a tool of terror—designed to crush the spirits of the Theocracy’s enemies and
ensure that they will never rise again.
