# Uvath'gahn

<!-- Source PDF page 37 -->

U
vath'gahn is a nation inhabited solely by the
Iverken, a sentient and mobile species of
plant-like beings. Located in a large swath of
unspoiled land, the forests of Uvath'gahn are
said to be all connected to Sisulgar'maravan,
the main god of the Iverken and head of their
pantheon.As a result, the Iverken worship
Sisulgar'maravan and hold the forests in high regard,
protecting them fiercely from any who seek to exploit them.
As the only known iverken stronghold, Uvath'gahn is a
dangerous place for those who seek to exploit its resources.
However, the Iverken are generous to travelers and nomads
who respect the sanctity of their forests. Legendary wardens
likeAval'dun walk through the forests, protecting the Iverken
and their home from harm. Uvath'gahn is a unique and awe-
inspiring nation, with a culture and society that revolves
around the protection and reverence of nature.
## Government

### Central government

The central government of Uvath'gahn is a unique and
organic system, as it is made up of every Iverken living within
the nation. This collective is driven by their shared ancestry
and loyalty to Sisulgar'maravan, the main god of the Iverken
pantheon. They act as a force of nature, always serving the
greater good of their deity and the well-being of their land.
There is no room for mercy or ulterior motives in their
decision-making process.
As a result, the government operates in a highly efficient
and decisive manner, as every member works together
towards a common goal. There is no hierarchy or central
authority, and decisions are made through a consensus-based
system that values the input of every member. Despite this
lack of formal structure, the Iverken are unified in their
purpose and have a strong sense of community.
However, there are some Iverken who choose to leave the
nation to explore and gather information about the world
beyond their borders. These individuals are highly respected
by their fellow Iverken, as they bring valuable knowledge and
insights back to their homeland. However, they also
understand the risks and challenges of leaving their
protective and familiar environment.
### Local Government

As the central government of Uvath'gahn is made up of every
Iverken living within the nation, there is no formal local
government system in place. Instead, decisions and policies
are made collectively by the Iverken as a whole, with every
member having a say in the matter.
However, there are smaller groups of Iverken within the
nation that work together on specific tasks or projects. These
groups operate on a more localized level, with members
focusing on issues and challenges specific to their region or
community. These groups may be formed based on a
particular skill set, interest, or need within the community.
Despite these smaller groups, there is no hierarchy or
formal power structure within the central or local
government of Uvath'gahn.All decisions are made through a
consensus-based system, with every member having an equal
say in the matter.
## People

### Iverken Workers

The Iverken workers are a crucial part of the Uvath'gahn
society. They are responsible for the day-to-day tasks that
keep the nation running smoothly. Iverken workers come in
all shapes and sizes, from the smaller and more nimble vine
workers to the larger and stronger tree workers.
The vine workers are known for their flexibility and quick
movements, and they are often tasked with weaving together
the smaller plants and vines that make up the intricate
infrastructure of the Iverken homes and buildings. The tree
workers, on the other hand, are better suited for heavy lifting
and manual labor. They can often be seen carrying large logs
or boulders with ease, and are responsible for constructing
the larger structures in the nation.
All Iverken workers have a deep connection to nature and
the earth. They understand the importance of their work in
maintaining the balance of the ecosystem, and take great
pride in their abilities. They are often led by the Iverken
Wardens, who oversee the various projects and ensure that
the workers are working efficiently and safely.
### Iverken Wardens

The Iverken Wardens are a group of highly respected and
skilled Iverken who have dedicated themselves to protecting
the forests and the Iverken way of life. They are the guardians
of Uvath'gahn, responsible for defending the nation against
any threat that may arise, whether it be from humans,
monsters, or other forces of nature.
The Wardens are chosen based on their skill, experience,
and dedication to the cause. They undergo rigorous training
in both combat and strategy, learning to use their unique
abilities to their fullest potential. They are experts in forest
survival, tracking, and hunting, and are well-versed in the
traditions and mythology of the Iverken.
The Wardens work together closely, using their combined
skills and knowledge to keep the nation safe. They patrol the
forests, monitoring the wildlife and keeping an eye out for any
signs of danger. They also act as guides and mentors to
younger Iverken, passing down their knowledge and skills to
the next generation.
In times of crisis, the Wardens are called upon to act
swiftly and decisively. They work together to develop
strategies and tactics to combat any threat, and are willing to
risk their own lives to protect their home and people.

### Iverken Drift Seeds

Iverken Drift Seeds is the name given to the Iverken who
choose to wander beyond the borders of their homeland and
explore the world. These wanderers are driven by a natural
curiosity and a desire to expand their knowledge of the world
and the different cultures and creatures that inhabit it.
Drift Seeds are often seen as emissaries of their homeland,
as they carry with them the unique knowledge and wisdom of
their people. They are respected by other Iverken as well as
by outsiders, and are often sought out as guides or advisors.
Drift Seeds are known for their resourcefulness and
adaptability, as they must be able to survive in a wide variety
of environments and situations. They often form close bonds
with other travelers, and may even establish temporary
communities in the wild.
Despite their wanderlust, Drift Seeds always retain a deep
reverence for their homeland and their culture. Many choose
to return to Uvath'gahn after years of exploration, bringing
with them new ideas and experiences that can benefit their
people.
### The Avatar Of Sisilgur'Maravan

The central tree of Uvath Gahn is the physical manifestation
of Sisulgar'maravan, the father of soil and the main god of
the Iverken. This massive old oak tree stands taller and wider
than any other tree in the forest, and its roots stretch deep
into the earth, connecting it to all other trees and plants in
the area. The trunk and branches of the tree are covered in
thick moss, and vines and flowers grow along its bark.
The Iverken believe that the central tree is the source of all
life and power in Uvath Gahn. The tree is said to have the
ability to communicate with the other trees and plants in the
forest, and it can even control them to some extent. In times
of need, the tree can use its power to summon natural
disasters, such as storms or earthquakes, to defend the forest
and its inhabitants.
TheAvatar of Sisulgar'maravan is the physical
embodiment of the central tree's power. It appears as a
humanoid figure made entirely of leaves, vines, and flowers.
Its body glows with a faint green light, and it emanates a
powerful aura of life and energy. TheAvatar is fiercely
protective of the forest and its inhabitants, and it will stop at
nothing to defend them from harm.
The Iverken hold the central tree and itsAvatar in the
highest regard, and they believe that it is their duty to protect
and serve them. The tree is a symbol of their connection to
the natural world, and they believe that its power flows
through them. The Wardens of Uvath Gahn often consult
with the central tree and itsAvatar for guidance and support,
and the Iverken workers use its power to cultivate the land
and ensure the forest's prosperity.
## Military

The Iverken of Uvath Gahn do not have a traditional military
in the sense that other nations do. Instead, they rely on their
wardens and workers to defend their territory and way of life.
The wardens act as protectors and enforcers of the law, while
the workers are skilled in crafting weapons and tools from
the natural resources around them.
In times of great need, the central tree of Uvath Gahn,
which is the avatar of Sisulgar'maravan, may use its immense
power over nature to aid the wardens in defending against
external threats. This power is not used lightly and is
reserved for only the most dire of circumstances.
## Geography

Uvath Gahn is a nation with diverse and beautiful geography.
To the west, there are towering mountain ranges with snow-
capped peaks that stretch as far as the eye can see. These
mountains are a formidable natural barrier that separates
Uvath Gahn from neighboring regions. The mountains are
also the source of several rivers that flow into the nation,
providing fresh water to the inhabitants.
To the east of the mountains lie vast expanses of thick
forests. These forests are home to a variety of flora and
fauna, and the Iverken who inhabit them. The forests are
connected by an intricate network of trails and pathways that
make it easy for the Iverken to travel through them.
In the heart of Uvath Gahn lies an enormous lake. The lake
is so large that it can be seen from the mountains to the west.
The lake is fed by several rivers and streams and is home to a
wide variety of fish and other aquatic creatures. The lake is
also surrounded by lush greenery and is a popular
destination for travelers and tourists.
## Major cities

### Uvath'gahn

The capital city of Uvath Gahn, also named Uvath Gahn, is
located in the heart of the nation, surrounded by dense
forests and near the shores of the enormous lake. The city is
built around the central Tree of Uvath Gahn, theAvatar of
Sisulgar'maravan, and the most sacred site in the nation.
The city is designed to blend in with the surrounding
forests, with buildings made of natural materials such as
wood, stone, and woven grass. The streets wind around the
roots of the central tree, creating a maze-like layout that can
be confusing for outsiders. However, the locals navigate the
city with ease and use the tree as a central reference point.
The city has a bustling market where Iverken traders from
all over the nation come to sell their wares. The market is
filled with the scents of exotic fruits, Magical herbs, and
spices, as well as the sounds of merchants haggling and
Iverken chatting.
### Shill'ga'Vath

Shill'ga'Vath is a sprawling city located in the eastern region
of Uvath Gahn. Unlike human cities, it does not have any
structures made of stone or metal. Instead, the city is built
from living trees and plants that have been carefully woven
and grown together over centuries. The buildings are a mix of
open-air platforms and more enclosed structures made of
woven branches and leaves.
The city is centered around a massive tree known as the
Heart of Shill'ga'Vath. This tree is believed to be one of the
oldest and most powerful iverken in all of Uvath Gahn. It is
said that the Heart can communicate with other trees and
plants in the city, allowing it to monitor the health and
wellbeing of its citizens.

### Davoth'ur

Davoth'ur is a small city located in the northeast region of
Uvath'gahn, surrounded by dense forests and located near
the foothills of the mountains. Unlike the bustling city of
Shill'ga'Vath, Davoth'ur is known for its quiet and peaceful
atmosphere, which is perfect for contemplation and
meditation.
The city is populated mainly by Iverken, who have built
their homes and structures in harmony with nature, using the
natural resources of the forests and mountains around them.
The buildings are made of wood and stone, and are
decorated with intricate carvings depicting natural scenes
and mythological creatures from the Iverken folklore.
At the center of the city, there is a large tree, which serves
as a meeting place and a source of spiritual guidance for the
Iverken community. The tree is said to be the material plane
home of a powerful fae spirit, who provides wisdom and
guidance to those who seek it.
## Economy

As a society closely attuned to nature, the economy of Uvath
Gahn is largely centered around sustainable resource
management and the cultivation of the forest. The Iverken
Wardens oversee the harvesting of timber, hunting of game,
and fishing in the lake, all while carefully monitoring and
balancing the impact on the environment. In addition to these
primary industries, there are also some smaller-scale farming
communities on the outskirts of the forests, producing crops
like grains and fruits that are used to supplement the diets of
the iverken.
Uvath Gahn also has a small but thriving crafts industry,
producing goods like hand-carved wooden items, textiles
woven from plant fibers, and pottery made from local clays.
Trade with other nations is limited, as Uvath Gahn places a
high value on maintaining the integrity of their natural
resources. However, they do engage in some limited
exchange of goods, particularly with neighboring human
settlements, and some iverken drift seed travelers may bring
back rare items from other lands to trade.
## Religion

The iverken of Uvath Gahn have a diverse pantheon of gods,
each representing different aspects of nature and the cycle of
life. Their main god, Sisulgar'maravan, is seen as the father
of soil and the source of all life in the forests. The iverken
believe that he gave them the power to think and is often
depicted as a massive old oak tree with roots reaching to the
underworld and branches reaching into the void.
Arvan Gavath, the twin sun, is the god of light and dark,
representing the cycle of day and night, and providing the
iverken with sustenance. His image is that of a large gem set
in a flower with a glowing core.
Beathal, the lady of decay, is the goddess of death and
decay. She is seen as essential to the life cycle of the forests,
as she cleans up the fallen iverken and merges them with the
earth to provide nutrients for other plants. Her image is that
of a large dung beetle with the face of a woman on its back.
Its shell is gold and black, and her face is made of wood.
Kukhalran, the faceless rain, is the god of rain and the
unknown. He represents the shifting nature of the iverken
and the importance of rain to the forests and their survival.
His image is that of a mountain in the shape of a tree with
rivers as roots and clouds as leaves. In iverken mythology,
this tree is seen as the gateway to the celestial realm and the
representation of the water cycle.
The iverken of Uvath Gahn honor and respect all of their
gods, as they see each one as a vital part of the delicate
balance of nature that sustains their way of life.

## Culture

The culture of Uvath Gahn is deeply rooted in the connection
between the Iverken people and the natural world around
them. The Iverken value harmony with nature, and their way
of life revolves around living in balance with their
environment. This connection is reflected in all aspects of
their culture, including their art, music, and spirituality.Art
and craftsmanship are highly valued in Iverken culture, and

#### Music

Music and dance are also important parts of Iverken culture,
with many of their traditional songs and dances featuring
themes of nature and the cycles of life. The Iverken also have
a strong tradition of storytelling, with myths and legends that
explain the origins of the world and the importance of the
natural world.
One of the most famous sung legends is the "Ballad of
Sisilgur Maravan", which tells the story of the creation of the
world and the birth of the Iverken people. It describes how
Sisilgur Maravan, the father of soil, used his powers to create
the forests and mountains of Uvath Gahn, and how he then
breathed life into the Iverken, his children.
Another popular sung legend is the "Elegy of the Drift
Seeds", which tells the story of the Iverken travelers who
leave Uvath Gahn to explore the wider world. It describes
their adventures and encounters with other cultures, as well
as the challenges and dangers they face.
#### Festivals and holidays

Uvath Gahn is a nation with a deep reverence for nature and
the gods who created it. Therefore, many of their festivals
and ceremonies are centered around nature and religious
observances.
Festival of the First Bloom: This festival is held in early
spring to celebrate the first blooming of the flowers in the
forests. It is a time of great joy and renewal, and the Iverken
celebrate by decorating the trees with brightly colored
ribbons and holding feasts. The Hunt: This is a sacred
ceremony held by the Iverken Wardens, who hunt only what
they need for sustenance. It is a solemn affair, and the
Wardens give thanks to the animals for giving their lives to
sustain them.
#### Education

In Uvath Gahn, education is primarily oral and observational.
As a society deeply connected to nature, much of their
knowledge and wisdom is passed down through stories and
legends, often shared during community gatherings or
ceremonies.
Young iverken learn about their environment, culture, and
history by observing and participating in daily tasks alongside
their elders. For example, they learn how to hunt, fish, forage,
and cultivate crops by accompanying the hunters, fishers, and
farmers on their tasks.
Additionally, the iverken have a tradition of apprenticeship,
where young individuals who show a particular talent or
interest in a certain craft or skill are taken under the wing of
a master craftsman or artisan. The apprenticeship can last
for several years, during which the apprentice learns by
observing, assisting, and practicing alongside their master.
