# The Outerlands

<!-- Source PDF page 88 -->

T
he Outer Lands are a vast and mysterious
region located beyond the known territories of
the Shattered continent and Tahsatran. To the
far east lies the perilous Tahsatran Badlands,
an inhospitable expanse of sand where
gargantuan monsters roam and few who
venture there ever return. It is said that
somewhere beyond the Badlands lies the passage to the
fabled Throne of Vjalkraven, a legendary realm of power and
mystery.
To the far west are the uncharted waters of the Far Reach,
where intrepid sailors have reported the discovery of new
lands, replete with dense jungles and exotic creatures yet
unknown to the wider world. These islands remain largely
unexplored, and are rumored to hold ancient ruins and lost
treasures.
The people of the Shattered continent and Tahsatran are
renowned explorers, and many have set out to discover the
secrets of the Outer Lands. However, the dangers and
uncertainties of these distant regions make such journeys
perilous and fraught with uncertainty, and only the bravest
and most skilled adventurers dare to venture beyond the
known world.
## People

As nomads, the people of the Outer Lands are defined by
their constant movement and adaptability to their
environment.
### Nomads of the Tashatran Desert

The Nomads of the Tashatran Desert, for example, have
honed their survival skills over generations, mastering the art
of finding water and resources in an otherwise hostile
terrain. They are a people of deep spiritual beliefs, with a
reverence for magic and the natural world.
### the tribes of the Far Reach

the tribes of the Far Reach have learned to thrive in the dense
jungles of their homeland, living off the land and utilizing
their knowledge of the flora and fauna around them. The
Mayacan Humans and Yuan ti Tribes, in particular, have
developed unique cultures and traditions that reflect their
intimate relationship with the jungle.

### Huang Tai

Far into the eastern Badlands lies the kingdom of Huang Tai,
whose people bear distinct features reminiscent ofAsian
ancestry. They too are nomads, moving their camps across
the desert in search of resources and sustenance. However,
unlike the more primitive tribes of the Far Reach and
Tashatran Desert, the people of Huang Tai have built a
kingdom that rivals the great empires of the Shattered
continent, with a highly structured society and advanced
technologies.
## Military

The militaries of the Outer Lands vary greatly depending on
the culture and geography of the region. However, most of
them share a common thread of being highly adaptive and
mobile, reflecting the nomadic lifestyles of their people.
The Nomads of the Tashatran Desert have a fierce
reputation as warriors, with each tribe having their own
distinctive fighting style. They are skilled in the use of ranged
weapons like bows and arrows, and their mobility allows
them to strike quickly and disappear into the desert. They are
also known for their use of magic in battle, particularly in the
form of sandstorms and illusions.
The tribes of the Far Reach are similarly mobile and
adaptive, with their warriors utilizing the dense jungle to
their advantage. They are experts in guerrilla warfare, using
hit-and-run tactics to wear down their enemies. They are also
adept at using traps and ambushes, making them formidable
opponents even for larger, more organized armies.
The kingdom of Huang Tai, on the other hand, has a more
structured military organization. Their soldiers are well-
trained and disciplined, with a hierarchy of officers and
commanders. They have developed advanced technologies
and unknown magics like siege engines,crossbows and black
powder, making them a force to be reckoned with on the
battlefield. They also have a strong navy, which they use to
control trade routes and protect their coastline.
## Geography

The geography of the Outer Lands is defined by its extremes
and contrasts, with the Badlands to the east and the Rising
Waterfalls to the far west, with the jungle islands beyond
them.
The Tashatran Badlands are a vast expanse of sand and
rock, stretching for hundreds of miles in every direction. The
climate is harsh and unforgiving, with scorching
temperatures during the day and freezing cold at night. The
landscape is dotted with rocky outcroppings, deep ravines,
and shifting sand dunes, making travel treacherous and
unpredictable. Gargantuan monsters roam these wastes,
posing a constant threat to any who dare venture into their
territory.
To the far west lie the Rising Waterfalls, where the sea
rises to form towering cliffs and cascading waterfalls. These
natural wonders are a marvel to behold, and have inspired
countless stories and legends. Beyond the Waterfalls lie the
jungle islands, a vast and largely unexplored region of dense
jungles, towering trees, and winding rivers. The climate is
humid and tropical, with frequent rain showers and
occasional tropical storms. The islands are teeming with
wildlife, ranging from small primates and colorful birds to
ferocious predators and venomous snakes.
## Major cities

The Outer Lands are a vast and largely unexplored region,
and as such, there are few major cities in the area. However,
there are a few notable settlements that stand out as centers
of trade, culture, and civilization.
One of the largest cities in the region is located in the
kingdom of Huang Tai, far to the east beyond the Badlands.
This city, also known as Huang Tai, is a marvel of
architecture and engineering, with towering walls, grand
palaces, and intricate water systems. It is home to a bustling
market district, where traders from across the region come to
buy and sell goods. The city is also known for its universities
and schools, where scholars and students come to study the
arts, sciences, and magic.
## Economy

he economy of the Outer Lands is diverse and complex, with
different regions and peoples engaged in various forms of
trade, production, and exchange.
In the Tashatran Desert, the economy is largely based on
subsistence agriculture, nomadic herding, and the trade of
goods such as spices, herbs, and textiles. The nomads of the
desert are skilled in survival and resourcefulness, and rely on
the harsh environment to provide for their basic needs. They
also engage in trade with neighboring regions, exchanging
their goods for items such as weapons, tools, and luxury
items.
In the Far Reach, the economy is centered around fishing,
forestry, and mining. The coast is rich in fish and seafood,
which are caught and sold to markets across the region. The
vast jungles of the Far Reach provide a wealth of timber and
other forest products, which are harvested and traded for
various goods. Finally, the mountains contain rich deposits of
valuable minerals, such as gold and silver, which are
extracted and sold for profit.
In Huang Tai, the economy is centered around agriculture,
manufacturing, and trade. The fertile lands surrounding the
city are used to grow a variety of crops, including rice, wheat,
and various fruits and vegetables. The city is also known for
its skilled craftsmen, who produce a range of goods including
pottery, textiles, and metalwork. Finally, the city is a major
center of trade, with merchants from across the region
coming to buy and sell goods in its bustling markets.

## Religion

The religion of the Outer Lands is a fascinating and diverse
topic, reflecting the rich cultural and historical traditions of
the region. While there is no one dominant religion, it is clear
that many different belief systems coexist and interact with
each other, creating a dynamic and complex religious
landscape. For example, in the Tashatran Desert, the
nomadic tribes follow animistic or shamanistic beliefs, seeing
the natural world as infused with spiritual power and
significance. This perspective emphasizes the
interconnectedness of all things and encourages people to
respect and honor the spirits and deities associated with the
land and environment. in the Far Reach, various religious
traditions are practiced by different groups, reflecting the
unique cultures and histories of each people. The Mayacan,
for example, may follow ancestral or nature-based religions,
while the Yuan ti tribes may worship serpent deities or
engage in ritual sacrifice. In coastal towns, there may be
some influence from religions of neighboring regions, such as
the worship of sea gods or goddesses. In Huang Tai, the
dominant religion emphasizes the worship of a pantheon of
deities, with the Emperor serving as a religious as well as
political leader. This religion places great importance on
ancestor worship, with the belief that ancestors can influence
the lives of their living descendants. The Emperor and other
high-ranking officials may also perform various rituals and
offerings to ensure the prosperity and well-being of the
nation.
