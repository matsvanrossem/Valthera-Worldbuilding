# Legendary
Corrupted Black Orbs

The Heart of the Prime Material Plane

Wondrous Item (Artifact), Requires Attunement

Wondrous Item (Artifact), Requires Attunement

Description: The Corrupted Black Orbs are ancient and cursed artifacts, once created to power the Null Arcanum
Constructs of Nash Retah. Crafted by the greatest minds of the ancient city-state, they were designed to absorb and
magic—until their corruption by the eldritch presence of Drastragha, the Dreamless Sleep. These black, head-sized orbs
hum with a terrible energy, an insidious pull that warps the very fabric of reality around them.
Touched by the hands of those who dare to hold them, the orbs spread a creeping madness that consumes the mind and
twists the body. Upon activation, they manifest an even darker power, one that bends life itself into an ever-growing,
uncontrollable distortion. The orb's presence warps its surroundings, casting an aura of dread, and is said to twist all that
it touches into nightmare-like aberrations.

Description: The Heart of the Prime Material Plane is an ancient, almost mythical artifact, forged at the
dawn of the multiverse. It embodies the balance of existence, serving as a conduit between the planes
and the primal forces that sustain the fabric of reality itself. Its radiance reflects the pulse of life and the
silent undercurrent of death, maintaining harmony between the dimensions, while also holding the
potential to unravel everything in a heartbeat.
The artifact is a crystalline gemstone, its size that of a human heart, with veins of iridescent light running
across its multifaceted surface. These veins shimmer in hues of blues, greens, purples, and golds,
pulsating in a steady rhythm, like the heart of the multiverse itself. Though beautiful, the Heart exudes a
power that both attracts and terrifies—its raw primal energy is capable of altering reality itself.

Properties:
Corrupted Aura:

Properties:

Any creature that touches the Corrupted Black Orb must make a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw or suffer a surge of
madness as Drastragha’s whispers tear at their mind. On a failed save, the creature becomes mad for 24 hours,
experiencing vivid, terrifying hallucinations and losing all sense of time. This madness lingers for long after the effect
ends, leaving the victim with permanent anxiety, paranoia, or delusions that last for 1d6 days.
While the orb is in possession, those nearby (within 30 feet) suffer a slight disturbance in reality—distorted sounds,
brief flashes of nightmarish visions, or sudden feelings of disconnection from the world around them.

Aetheric Conduit:

Twisting Presence (Activation):
Once activated (either through ritual or an arcane means), the Corrupted Black Orb unleashes a dreadful presence that
twists life around it.
Creatures attempting to cast spells near the orb must make a DC 16 Constitution saving throw or feel their magic
unravel and dissipate, causing spells to fail or become horribly warped.
The radius of its effect grows by 5 feet every hour it remains activated, with a maximum radius of 60 feet. Within this
area, all living creatures must make a DC 18 Constitution saving throw or begin to mutate into horrifying, monstrous
forms. The transformation is subtle at first—slight physical changes or discomfort—but grows worse over time.
• First day: Minor mutations begin—longer claws, changes in skin pigmentation, or eyes that glow with an unnatural
light. These mutations cause no immediate harm but are disorienting. The creature gains a disadvantage on
Wisdom (Perception) checks.
• Second day: The mutations intensify—flesh warps, muscles distort unnaturally, and their very being begins to take
on grotesque shapes. These creatures now suffer disadvantage on all Dexterity and Charisma-based skill checks.
• Third day (and beyond): At this stage, the mutations have fully taken hold. Creatures who failed their saving
throws suffer permanent, horrific changes, becoming Twisted Abominations. Their bodies no longer function in a
natural way, and they are effectively treated as Aberrations. Each transformed creature gains a random aberrant
trait from the Aberration Traits table (e.g., extra limbs, a mouth where none should be, ethereal shadows clinging
to them, or becoming partially translucent).
Additionally, the creatures begin to suffer intense psychic damage (3d6 every day of exposure) as their mind and body
are twisted together. Once this transformation completes, the creature is effectively under the influence of Drastragha,
their consciousness fading into nothingness, consumed by the entity’s dreamless slumber.

Madness of the Dreamless Sleep:
The Corrupted Black Orb is linked to the mind-warping power of Drastragha. Once attuned, the user’s mind becomes
susceptible to the whispers of this Great Old One. They begin to experience disturbing dreams and visions of a vast,
formless void where time, life, and death hold no meaning. Each night spent with the orb brings the user closer to losing
themselves completely to Drastragha's influence.
• 1-3 Days of Attunement: The user experiences nightmares of non-existence and the endless void. They suffer
disadvantage on Intelligence-based checks and saving throws.
• 4-7 Days of Attunement: The user begins to question their reality. The boundaries between dreams and waking
life blur, and they hear whispers of forgotten truths. The user gains a short-term madness (determined by the
DM) and suffers disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws.
• 8+ Days of Attunement: The user starts to slip into madness. The whispers of Drastragha become louder, and the
user may attempt to mimic Drastragha’s power, causing them to use psychic damage abilities involuntarily. If they
are forced to roll a d20, on a roll of 1-5, they may inadvertently warp or damage reality itself.

Void-Touched Effects (Passive):
While the orb is in the presence of living beings, its cursed aura can reach beyond the immediate area, causing the air
itself to grow thick with unnatural dread. This effect radiates up to 60 feet from the orb. Creatures within this area are
unable to rest fully. Any attempt to sleep within range results in troubled sleep filled with horrifying visions of the
Dreamless Sleep, causing creatures to suffer disadvantage on any Wisdom (Perception) checks during their rest and
providing no benefit from a long rest.
• Twisted Dreams: Any creature attempting to sleep within 60 feet of the orb must make a DC 16 Wisdom saving
throw. On a failure, they gain the Nightmare condition, which causes them to suffer psychic damage (2d10) the
following day.

Curse:
The Corrupted Black Orb is not only a powerful artifact—it is a curse that spreads as it is used. Any creature attuned to it
risks becoming possessed by Drastragha, an ancient slumbering entity. The corruption grows, and their very soul
becomes a tool for the entity’s will.
• Corruption of the Self: After 1 month of attunement, the user’s body and mind begin to deteriorate. They suffer
permanent madness (such as paranoia, delusions of grandeur, or catatonia) and begin to lose a portion of their
memories each day, forgetting important parts of their past.
• Unraveling Mind: After 3 months of attunement, the user’s identity begins to slip away entirely. They may begin
to lose control of their own body, feeling detached from their actions. The character gains the Possessed
condition, becoming an unwilling puppet of Drastragha. They are still able to act in combat, but the eldritch
influence will manipulate them, causing them to attack friends or even sabotage the party’s objectives when the
whispers grow too powerful.

Destruction of the Orb:
The Corrupted Black Orb can only be destroyed by severing the bond between the orb and Drastragha. To do so, the orb
must be brought into the heart of Nash Retah’s ruins, where the Grand Nullifier was originally created. It requires an
intense ritual involving magic from an opposing force—either a Divine Being, Celestial Energy, or an artifact with the
opposite properties (such as The Heart of the Prime Material Plane). Only through these means can the orb be cleansed
and destroyed, breaking its hold over reality and removing the presence of Drastragha from the mortal world.

The Heart allows the user to manipulate the very essence of existence, harnessing the primal forces that
define the universe. Upon attunement, the wielder gains the following abilities:
1. Reality Stabilization:
The Heart can be used to stabilize reality itself in moments of great danger. As a reaction, the
wielder can use the Heart to negate any major reality-warping effect (e.g., spells like Time Stop,
Plane Shift, or reality-bending abilities). This immediately restores the affected area to its natural
state.
○ Drawback: Each use of the Heart causes immense strain on the body, as the primal forces
rip through the user’s body. After using this ability, the wielder must succeed on a DC 18
Constitution saving throw or suffer 2d10 necrotic damage. If this damage reduces the user
to 0 hit points, they are knocked unconscious and cannot regain hit points for 1 hour.
2. Planeswalking:
The Heart enables the wielder to instantly travel to other planes of existence. The user can
teleport to any plane they are familiar with, though the journey is taxing.
○ Drawback: Every time the wielder uses this ability, their body is flooded with primal
energies as they tear through the planar fabric. After the teleportation, the user suffers 1
level of exhaustion and must make a DC 20 Constitution saving throw or take 3d6 force
damage from the energy backlash.
3. Restoration of Life:
The wielder can use the Heart to restore life to any creature that has been dead for no longer than
a century, regardless of how much damage the body has sustained.
○ Drawback: The intense primal forces of the Heart disrupt the natural order of life. Each use
of this ability drains 1d6 hit points from the user. If the wielder has fewer than 10 hit points
remaining after using this ability, they immediately suffer 1d10 necrotic damage and begin
to feel weak and disconnected from their own body, requiring a long rest to recover.
4. Balancing Force:
The Heart emanates an aura of stability, affecting all within a 30-foot radius. This aura nullifies
magical disturbances and prevents catastrophic reality-warping actions.
○ Drawback: Maintaining the aura while in a volatile situation causes painful strain. While the
aura is active, the user suffers 1d4 necrotic damage at the start of each of their turns. This
damage increases to 2d4 if the user has used the Heart’s abilities more than once in the last
24 hours.

Cosmic Burden:
With the Heart’s power comes an undeniable cost to the body and soul. The primal energies coursing
through the user’s body after every use begin to slowly twist and warp their form, leaving a permanent
mark on both their appearance and vitality.
• Body of the Nexus: Every time the wielder uses the Heart, their body suffers as primal forces rage
within them. With each activation of the Heart, the user must make a DC 15 Constitution saving
throw or suffer one of the following:
○ Severe Burning: Their skin erupts in painful, glowing veins of magical energy. They take 1d8
fire damage immediately, and their movement speed is reduced by 10 feet until they finish
a long rest.
○ Bone Shattering Pain: Their bones feel as if they’re being compressed and expanded. The
user gains disadvantage on their next attack roll, saving throw, or ability check.
○ Corrupted Growth: The user's body mutates slightly each time they use the Heart. After
three uses, their body begins to sprout primal appendages, such as claws or extra fingers.
These mutations grant them advantage on Strength checks, but they become an unsettling
sight to behold—creatures who look at the user must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw or
be frightened for 1 minute.
• Primal Slumber: The more the Heart is used, the closer the wielder comes to slipping into a
permanent sleep of primal oblivion, a form of stasis where their body and mind are lost to the
primal forces they wield. After 5 uses within a 24-hour period, the wielder must make a DC 18
Wisdom saving throw or fall into a deep, coma-like sleep. During this time, they are completely
unaware of their surroundings, unable to act or communicate, and may not be awakened until
they receive a Wish or True Resurrection spell.

Cosmic Burden (Continued):
The wielder's soul begins to fragment after each use of the Heart. Every time they use it, they lose part
of their connection to the world of the living and slip closer to an unknowable fate, at the mercy of the
primal forces that flow through the Heart.
1. Fragmented Soul: Every time the wielder uses the Heart, they must make a DC 18 Charisma
saving throw. On a failed save, they lose a portion of their memories. This could be trivial—
forgetting the name of a loved one—or significant—losing an important skill or talent. With
enough failures, the user’s memories may become incomplete and they might forget their very
reason for wielding the Heart.
2. Primal Madness: As the wielder draws more primal energy from the Heart, their mind becomes
increasingly unstable. After each use, there is a cumulative 10% chance that the user will be driven
into a fit of primal rage for 1 minute. During this time, they attack the nearest creature with
reckless abandon, using their maximum strength and abilities until the fit subsides or they are
knocked unconscious.

wielder can twist reality itself to create new realms. However, creating new worlds comes at a terrible

• New World Creation: As an action, the wielder can use the Heart to create a new plane of

causes the Heart’s primal energy to tear into the wielder’s body, causing 3d10 force damage. If
the user survives, they must immediately make a DC 25 Constitution saving throw or take
permanent damage to one of their abilities (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence,
Wisdom, or Charisma) that can never be healed except through greater restoration or similar
magic.

Destruction of the Heart:
The Heart’s destruction is not just a simple matter of breaking the gem. The primal energies housed
within it are so potent that its annihilation would tear apart the very fabric of the Prime Material Plane
itself.
To destroy the Heart, a user must expose it to the Heart of the Void—an entity or force that exists
beyond the boundaries of existence. Only through a great sacrifice—such as the user’s own life force or
soul—can the Heart be shattered. The destruction would cause a cataclysmic rift, potentially unraveling
the entire reality or tearing the user’s own existence apart. Once destroyed, the Heart cannot be
remade, and the Prime Material Plane itself may begin to collapse into chaos.

Belt of Fire Giant Strength
Wondrous Item (belt), very rare, requires attunement (curse)
While wearing this belt, your Strength score becomes 25, as normal for a Belt of Fire Giant
Strength.
However, the belt was forged in a giant crucible that demanded dominion through force,
and that hunger still burns within it.

Curse Effects
1. The Giant’s Temper
The wearer’s blood runs hot with barely restrained fury.
• When you take damage, you must succeed on a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw or
become provoked until the end of your next turn.
• While provoked:
○ You have advantage on Strength-based skill checks
○ You have disadvantage on Wisdom and Charisma checks
○ You must move toward and attack the nearest creature that got damaged if
able
This is not magical compulsion, but an overwhelming instinct.

2. Heat of Dominion
The belt despises weakness.
• If you retreat from combat, surrender, or allow an enemy who bested you to escape,
you take 10d6 fire damage at the end of the encounter.
• This damage ignores resistance, but not immunity.
Once attuned, the belt cannot be willingly removed.
• Removing it requires:
○ Remove Curse cast at 6th level or higher, or
○ A ritual involving quenching the belt in the blood of a slain fire giant leader
Until then:
• Attempts to remove it fail
• The belt tightens painfully if forced (3d6 fire damage)
